How to Budget a ₱20,000 Monthly Salary

Easy Budget Tracker guides · Updated October 22, 2026

A ₱20,000 monthly salary can go a long way when every peso has a job. The goal is not to be perfect. It is to decide your plan before the month starts, so spending does not decide it for you. The figures below are an example only. Rent, food and transport vary a lot between cities, so change the numbers to match your life.

Step 1: Start with your take-home pay

Use the amount that actually reaches you after deductions, not the gross figure. Check your payslip. If your income changes each month, use your lowest typical month and treat anything above it as a bonus to allocate later.

Step 2: List the fixed costs first

Write down everything that must be paid on a schedule: rent or boarding, electricity, water, internet, mobile load or plan, transport passes, insurance, and any loan or installment payments. Add the due date next to each one. Fixed costs show you how much money is truly free.

Step 3: Try an example split

GroupExampleWhat goes in it
Needs₱11,000Rent or boarding, utilities, groceries, transport, mobile and internet
Wants₱5,000Eating out, shopping, streaming, hobbies, gifts
Savings and debt₱4,000Emergency fund, extra loan or card payments

If your needs already take more than ₱11,000, shrink the wants first and keep even a small savings amount, such as ₱500, so the habit starts. You can read more about this style of split in our 50/30/20 rule guide.

Step 4: Pay yourself first

Move your savings amount to a separate account on payday, before you spend. Whatever remains is your spending money for the month.

Step 5: Set limits for flexible categories

Groceries, eating out and transport are the categories that drift. Give each one a monthly limit and check it once a week. A weekly check takes two minutes and catches overspending while you can still fix it.

Step 6: Plan for irregular money

If you receive a 13th-month pay or a bonus, decide where it goes before it arrives, for example half to your emergency fund and half to a planned expense. Unplanned windfalls disappear quickly.

This guide is general information, not financial advice. Amounts are illustrative.
