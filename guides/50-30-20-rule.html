The 50/30/20 Budget Rule Explained

Easy Budget Tracker guides · Updated October 22, 2026

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to divide your after-tax income: 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and debt repayment. It became widely known through the book All Your Worth by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi. It is a starting point, not a law.

What counts as a need, a want or savings?

  • Needs: things you must pay to live and work, such as housing, utilities, basic groceries, transport to work, insurance and minimum debt payments.
  • Wants: things that improve life but are not essential, such as dining out, subscriptions, travel and shopping.
  • Savings and debt: emergency fund, retirement, other goals, and any payments above the minimum on loans or cards.

Example with ₱30,000 take-home pay

GroupShareAmount
Needs50%₱15,000
Wants30%₱9,000
Savings and debt20%₱6,000

When the rule does not fit

If rent and food take most of your pay, 50% for needs may be unrealistic. Many people use 60/20/20 or 70/20/10 instead. What matters is that the savings share is not zero. If 20% feels impossible, begin with 5% and raise it every few months.

How to use it in practice

  1. Write down your monthly take-home pay.
  2. Multiply by 0.5, 0.3 and 0.2 to get your target amounts.
  3. Sort last month's spending into the three groups.
  4. Compare, then trim the group that is over its share, usually wants first.

The Easy Budget Tracker keeps bills, spending and savings in separate groups, so you can see your split at a glance. For a worked example on a smaller income, see how to budget a ₱20,000 salary.
